# CONSTRUCTION COST / CONTRACT / QS / PROCUREMENT — COMPLETE JOB LIST (India primary)

**Sourcing note up front, because it changes what you can trust below.** I verified from primary or near-primary sources: the IS 1200 part list (BIS/CED 44 index), RICS NRM structure (rics.org), SCL Protocol 2nd ed. methods, FIDIC 14.6/14.7 timings, BOCW Cess Act s.3(1), CPWD DSR-2023 + AOR volumes (Railway Board adoption order), GFR 2017 Rule 149/161/173, Arbitration Act s.12(5)/Seventh Schedule + *Perkins Eastman*, Contract Act s.74 case law, GST entry 3(ii) at 18%. I could **not** retrieve: the NCO-2015 Volume II-B 8-digit code table (every mirror I tried — labour.gov.in, dge.gov.in, ncs.gov.in — returned 404, a connection reset, or a redirect to a site homepage), the verbatim statutory text of RERA s.4(2)(l)(D) (indiacode.nic.in returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch; I have the provision from multiple ICAI-hosted and practitioner sources that quote it consistently), and the official CPWD Works Manual 2022 body text (the NDMC-hosted "CPWD Works Manual 2022.pdf" is a 91-page scanned issuing circular + SOP, not the manual). Everything I could not confirm is marked **NOT FOUND** or **NOT VERIFIED** rather than filled in.

**On NCO-2015 codes specifically.** NCO-2015 is an 8-digit system mapped onto ISCO-08 by adding 2 digits to the ISCO 4-digit unit group ([DGE](https://dge.gov.in/nat?page=4)). I verified exactly one relevant family from the official Vol-I code structure: **2142 Civil Engineers**, with sub-codes of the form 2142.0100 Civil Engineer General, 2142.0400 Civil Engineer Irrigation, 2142.0800 Airport Engineer. I did **not** find a "Quantity Surveyor" title anywhere in NCO-2015 — ESCO maps quantity surveyor to **ISCO-08 2149** ([crosswalk](https://worldoftaxonomy.com/codes/esco_occupations/0cca32c2-9308-4927-adb2-14771ab787f0)), and the older **NCO-2004** table carries *"Estimator, Engineering" at 2149.84* ([microdata.gov.in conversion table](https://microdata.gov.in/NADA43/index.php/catalog/81/download/743)). So for every job below I give the ISCO-08-aligned family that NCO-2015 must inherit, flagged as **inferred**, and the exact 8-digit sub-code as **NOT FOUND**. Do not ship a code from this list as authoritative; the one file that settles it is NCO-2015 Vol II-B.

## A. QUANTITY SURVEYING AND ESTIMATION

### A1. Quantity Surveyor (QS)
- **Indian titles:** QS; "QS & Billing"; on PSU/government side the function is split across *Estimator* and *Billing Engineer* and the standalone "QS" title is mostly private-sector, developer, and PMC.
- **NCO-2015:** 8-digit code **NOT FOUND**. Family inferred **2149** (ISCO-08 "Engineering professionals n.e.c.", to which ESCO maps quantity surveyor); NCO-2004 analogue 2149.84 "Estimator, Engineering".
- **Day to day:** Takes off quantities from drawings (manually or from a model), writes measured item descriptions, prices them, issues BOQs for tender, scrutinises contractors' rates, measures work in progress against the BOQ, values variations, prepares interim valuations, settles final accounts, and maintains the cost-vs-budget position.
- **Deliverables:** BOQ, bills of quantities with preambles, schedules of rates, taking-off sheets / dimension sheets, abstract of cost, interim valuations, variation valuations, final account statement, cost reports.
- **Employers:** Developers (DLF, Godrej, Prestige), PMC/consultancies (CBRE, JLL, Colliers, Turner & Townsend, RLB, Currie & Brown, Gleeds), EPC contractors (L&T, Tata Projects, Shapoorji Pallonji, Afcons), architecture practices, and the Gulf diaspora market that absorbs most Indian QS output.
- **THE GATE:** **In India, none.** There is no statutory licence or reserved act for a quantity surveyor. IIQS is a professional body incorporated under s.25 of the Companies Act 1956 with student/associate/full/fellow grades; I found **no** source showing it confers chartered status or holds statutory recognition. Internationally the gate is contractual, not statutory: MRICS/FRICS is a client requirement, and RICS members are bound to the NRM suite. The real statutory gate near this work in India is **RERA's engineer certificate** (see Section F2) — and that gate names an *engineer*, not a quantity surveyor, which is exactly the Indian anomaly.

### A2. Estimator
- **Indian titles:** Estimator; Estimation Engineer; "Tendering & Estimation".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; NCO-2004 carried "Estimator, Engineering" **2149.84**.
- **Day to day:** Pre-bid. Reads the tender drawings and specifications, does take-off, builds or picks rates, loads the rate with overhead/profit/risk, prices the priced BOQ the bid goes out on, prepares the cost sheet management signs, and owns the bid's margin assumption.
- **Deliverables:** Priced BOQ, rate analysis sheets, estimate / abstract, basic rate sheets for materials at the site location, enquiry comparatives from vendors and subcontractors, bid cost sheet with margin, alternative/optional-item pricing.
- **Employers:** Contractors and subcontractors above all; also PMCs preparing client-side estimates and government departments preparing sanction estimates.
- **THE GATE:** None statutory. In the government chain the *estimate* has an administrative gate rather than a professional one: a sanction estimate is technically sanctioned by the competent authority at the delegated financial power, and CPWD works are to be estimated on **CPWD DSR-2023 (Vol I, Vol II) with Analysis of Rates for DSR (AOR Vol I, Vol II)** and CPWD Specifications ([Railway Board adoption order, 03.07.2025](https://indianrailways.gov.in/railwayboard/uploads/directorate/civil_engg/2025/2025_07_03%20Adoption%20of%20CPWD%20Delhi%20Schedule%20of%20Rates-2023%20(2).pdf)). Departing from the schedule requires a justified non-schedule rate analysis approved at a stated level — that approval, not a licence, is the gate.

### A3. Billing Engineer — **the distinctly Indian role**
- **Indian titles:** Billing Engineer; QS/Billing Engineer; "RA Bill Engineer". There is no clean Western equivalent; it is the site-resident half of a QS, carved out because Indian measurement is still contemporaneous and Measurement-Book-based rather than valuation-based.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**. Sits between inferred 2142 (Civil Engineers) and inferred 3112 (civil engineering technicians) depending on seniority.
- **Day to day:** Two directions. *Outward* (contractor side): records executed quantities, gets them joint-measured and signed by the client's engineer, raises the RA bill with measurement sheets and abutting drawings, chases certification, tracks retention/advance recovery/deductions. *Inward* (client/PMC side): checks the contractor's claimed quantities against the MB and drawings, test-checks measurements, certifies or reduces, and recommends payment. Also keeps the subcontractor bill and material reconciliation running.
- **Deliverables:** RA bills (running account bills), measurement sheets / abutting measurement statements, **Measurement Book (MB) entries**, joint measurement records, deviation statements, extra-item and substituted-item statements, material reconciliation statements (cement/steel issued vs theoretically consumed), hindrance register, certified bill with recoveries, final bill.
- **Employers:** Contractors (biggest employer by headcount), PMCs, developers, government PWDs.
- **THE GATE:** This is the strongest *contractual* gate in Indian construction and it names a person by designation. Under public-works practice no running-account or final bill may be passed except on recorded Measurement Book entries, and the MB is a formal record: the **Junior Engineer records** the measurements and the **Assistant Engineer / Engineer-in-Charge test-checks and check-measures** them before the Divisional Officer passes the bill (CPWD Works Manual / CPWA Code; the 2019 manual's SOP lists "Test checking of Measurements" at **Para 5.14** — [NDMC-hosted SOP to CPWD Works Manual 2019](https://ndmc.gov.in/departments/Departments/Finance/nodal_cell/SOP%20to%20CPWD%20Works%20Manual%202019.pdf)). I could **NOT** retrieve the 2022 manual's renumbered paragraph or the exact test-check percentages (the "CPWD Works Manual 2022" PDF hosted by NDMC is a scanned issuing circular, 91 pages, not the manual body) — **treat the percentages as NOT FOUND and read them out of the signed contract and the department's own current manual.** The MB is a legal record of fact: a wrong entry, once signed, is the basis for payment and for audit.

### A4. Measurement / BOQ Engineer
- **Indian titles:** BOQ Engineer; Measurement Engineer; "Take-off Engineer"; increasingly "5D/QTO Engineer" where a model exists.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred family 2149 / 3112.
- **Day to day:** Pure quantification. Takes off from 2D drawings or extracts from the model, writes or applies item descriptions to a measurement standard, reconciles drawing revisions into quantity deltas, maintains the quantity register that everything else prices.
- **Deliverables:** Take-off sheets, BOQ with item codes and units, quantity schedules per element/floor/zone, revision-impact quantity comparisons, model QTO exports with a stated mapping to BOQ items.
- **Employers:** QS consultancies, contractors' estimation cells, BIM service firms, offshore QS back-offices serving UK/Gulf clients.
- **THE GATE:** None statutory. The binding gate is **which measurement standard governs**, and it must be stated in the contract: in India **IS 1200** (27 parts; Part 1 Earthwork 1992, Part 2 Concrete works 1974, Part 3 Brickwork 1976, Part 4 Stone masonry 1976, Part 5 Formwork 1982, Part 6 Refractory 1974, Part 7 Hardware 1972, Part 8 Steel and iron work 1993, Part 9 Roof covering 1973, Part 10 Ceiling and linings 1973, Part 11 Paving/floor finishes/dado/skirting 1977, Part 12 Plastering and pointing 1976, Part 13 Whitewashing/colour washing/distempering/painting 1994, Part 14 Glazing 1984, Part 15 Painting/polishing/varnishing 1987, Part 16 Water and sewer lines 1979, Part 18 Demolition and dismantling 1974, Part 19 Water supply/plumbing/drains 1981, Part 20 Gas and oil pipelines 1981, Part 21 Wood-work and joinery 1973, Part 22 Materials 1982, Part 23 Piling 1988, Part 24 Well foundations 1983, Part 25 Tunnelling 1971, Part 26 Acid-resistant lining 1987, Part 27 Earthwork by mechanical appliances 1992, Part 28 Sound insulation 1992 — **Part 17 does not exist** in the series; [BIS CED 44 index](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/manifest.ced.44.html)). Several parts have been revised since (Part 5 and Part 1 show 2025 entries, Part 6 a 2024 entry on the BIS portal) — **the revision year in force is contract-specific and must be read, not assumed.** Internationally, **RICS NRM 2** for detailed measurement; RICS states the **NRM 2021 suite** supersedes the earlier editions and the old ones "should not be used on new projects" ([rics.org](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/construction-standards/nrm)). NRM 2 replaced SMM7.

### A5. Rate Analyst
- **Indian titles:** Rate Analyst; "Rate Analysis Engineer"; usually a function inside Estimation rather than a standalone post except in large PSU/consultancy rate cells and in SOR-making departments.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**.
- **Day to day:** Builds the unit rate from first principles: material coefficients (with wastage), labour coefficients and wage rates, plant/T&P hire or ownership cost, then the overhead-and-profit load. Collects market basic rates, derives non-schedule-item (NS item) rates for extras, defends them in negotiation and audit.
- **Deliverables:** Rate analysis sheets per item, basic rate schedules for materials and labour at the site, machinery hire-rate schedules, NS-item rate justifications, SOR revision proposals.
- **Employers:** PWDs and SOR-framing cells (CPWD, state PWDs, MES, Railways, NHAI, irrigation departments), PSU engineering divisions, contractors, consultancies.
- **THE GATE:** Administrative, not professional: an NS-item rate must be sanctioned by the competent authority. The published build-up convention is **materials + labour + tools & plant + contractor's profit and overheads**, with CPWD/DSR practice adding **CP&OH at 15%** (confirmed in another government SOR — Ladakh SOR-2024 states "contractor's profit and overheads @ 15% has been adopted"). **Caution:** one secondary example applies 22% on a shuttering item, so the percentage is **item- and SOR-specific, not a constant** — read the AOR volume. I could not retrieve the official AOR text; percentages beyond the 15% convention are **NOT VERIFIED**.

### A6. Cost Engineer
- **Indian titles:** Cost Engineer; "Project Controls Engineer" (where cost and planning are merged); in EPC, "Cost Control Engineer".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred 2142 or 2149.
- **Day to day:** Owns the cost baseline and the gap between committed, incurred and forecast. Maintains the cost breakdown structure, codes commitments and actuals to it, computes earned value, produces estimate-to-complete and estimate-at-completion, runs change control on the cost side, and reconciles cost to the accounting ledger.
- **Deliverables:** Cost breakdown structure, budget baseline, commitment register, cost report with EV metrics (BCWS/BCWP/ACWP, CV, SV, CPI, SPI, EAC, ETC), accruals, trend and forecast curves, change log with cost impact.
- **Employers:** EPC contractors, oil & gas / power / metals owners, PMCs, infrastructure SPVs and lenders' engineers.
- **THE GATE:** None statutory. The gate is contractual sign-off on the **change** that moves the baseline (see C1/C3) and, on lender-funded projects, the **Lenders' Independent Engineer's** certification of cost-to-complete as a drawdown condition — that certificate is a named-person deliverable under the financing documents. Also see **Chartered Engineer certificate** (Section F5).

## B. COST MANAGEMENT

### B1. Cost Manager / Cost Consultant
- **Indian titles:** Cost Manager; Cost Consultant; "QS Lead"; on PMC appointments often "Commercial Lead". In the Indian market this is largely the international-consultancy job title for the senior QS role.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred 2149, or 1323 (Construction managers) at the head-of-function level — **inferred, not verified.**
- **Day to day:** Client-side ownership of the money. Sets the cost plan at each design stage, tests design changes against it, advises on procurement route and contract form, runs tender evaluation on the commercial side, reports cost certainty and risk allowance drawdown to the client's board.
- **Deliverables:** Order-of-cost estimate, elemental cost plan, cost plan updates at each stage with reconciliation to the previous plan, cash-flow forecast, risk and contingency register with drawdown, tender report with recommendation, post-contract cost reports.
- **Employers:** Client-side consultancies, developers' in-house commercial teams, government project cells, funds and institutional investors.
- **THE GATE:** None statutory in India. The international standard framing is **RICS NRM 1** for order-of-cost estimating and cost planning (NRM 2021 suite in force; earlier editions withdrawn for new projects — [rics.org](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/construction-standards/nrm)). Where the client is a listed company or a fund, the gate migrates to the auditor and the capital-expenditure approval resolution, not to the cost manager.

### B2. Cost Planner
- **Indian titles:** Cost Planner; Pre-contract QS; "Design-stage QS".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**.
- **Day to day:** Prices incomplete design. Builds elemental cost plans from functional units, floor areas and elemental unit quantities before a BOQ is possible; benchmarks against completed projects; maintains the cost database that makes benchmarking possible; tracks design drift against the plan.
- **Deliverables:** Order-of-cost estimate (cost/m², cost per key, cost per bed, cost per MW), elemental cost plan (NRM 1 Group Elements), cost-plan reconciliation showing design/specification/market/quantity causes of movement, benchmark comparison, cost-limit advice.
- **Employers:** Consultancies, developers, public clients, architects with in-house cost teams.
- **THE GATE:** None. **RICS NRM 1** governs the structure where RICS applies. India has no equivalent published cost-planning standard that I could locate — **NOT FOUND**.

### B3. Value Engineer
- **Indian titles:** Value Engineer; "VE Lead"; "Design-to-Cost Engineer"; in government, Value Engineering / Value Analysis cells.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**.
- **Day to day:** Runs the function-cost-worth analysis: identifies high-cost functions, generates alternatives, costs them including whole-life effect, tests them against the brief and the specification, and gets the designers and the client to accept or reject each one with a number attached.
- **Deliverables:** VE workshop register, function analysis, VE option log with cost saving, risk and programme effect per option, accepted/rejected/parked status, and the savings reconciliation back into the cost plan.
- **Employers:** PMCs, contractors (especially in design-build and item-rate conversion), developers under margin pressure, public clients.
- **THE GATE:** None. A VE change that alters the design must still pass the **designer of record**, and that is a licensed gate: the structural design sign-off is the structural engineer's, and the building-plan sanction is the **licensed architect's under the Architects Act 1972** and the local building bye-laws — a VE saving that changes a sanctioned drawing requires a revised sanction. **(The Architects Act licensing point is from my own knowledge and was NOT VERIFIED against the statute in this session.)**

### B4. Cost Controller
- **Indian titles:** Cost Controller; Project Cost Controller; "Budget Controller". Common in EPC, energy, and foreign-owned contractors; less common in domestic building contracting, where the billing engineer plus the accounts department covers it.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**.
- **Day to day:** Gate-keeps spend against budget. Approves or blocks requisitions against available budget lines, codes every commitment, computes accruals at period close, explains variance to the project manager and to finance, and keeps the cost ledger reconcilable to the books.
- **Deliverables:** Cost control report, budget-vs-commitment-vs-actual-vs-forecast tables, variance analysis with causes, accrual schedules, cost transfer / budget reallocation notes, cost-to-complete.
- **Employers:** EPC and energy contractors, owners' project teams, PMCs, lenders' monitoring agencies.
- **THE GATE:** Internal delegation of authority — the named approver at each value band. Where the entity is listed, the **internal financial controls** attestation under the Companies Act makes that delegation auditable. For a RERA project, the controller's cost-incurred figure is what feeds the **engineer's certificate** in F2, which is a licensed gate.

### B5. Cash-flow / Budget Manager
- **Indian titles:** Cash-flow Manager; Project Finance & Budget Manager; in developer-side roles frequently merged into "Project Finance" or "Business Planning".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred 2411 (accountants) or 1211 (finance managers) depending on where it sits — **inferred, not verified.**
- **Day to day:** Converts the programme and the contract payment terms into dated money in and money out. Builds S-curves, times retention release and advance recovery, matches collections against construction spend, flags the month the project runs dry, and manages the drawdown schedule with lenders.
- **Deliverables:** Cash-flow forecast (committed, probable, stretch), S-curve, drawdown schedule, receipt-vs-spend reconciliation, retention and advance recovery schedule, funding gap statement.
- **Employers:** Developers, contractors' treasury/commercial, SPVs, lenders.
- **THE GATE:** For a residential real-estate project this is where the statutory gate bites hardest. Under RERA, 70% of amounts realised from allottees goes into a separate account, withdrawals must be **in proportion to the percentage of completion**, and each withdrawal must be **certified by an engineer, an architect and a chartered accountant in practice** — see Section F2 for the provision and who certifies what. A cash-flow plan that assumes free access to collections is unlawful for a registered project.

## C. CONTRACTS AND COMMERCIAL

### C1. Contracts Manager
- **Indian titles:** Contracts Manager; Contracts & Commercial Manager; in PSUs "Contracts Officer/Dy. General Manager (Contracts)".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred 1323 (construction managers) or 2611 where legally qualified.
- **Day to day:** Owns the contract as an instrument. Drafts and negotiates the conditions, the particular conditions and the appendices; issues and answers notices within the contractual time bars; administers variations, EOT and claims; manages subcontract back-to-back terms; keeps the correspondence that becomes the evidence.
- **Deliverables:** Contract agreements and particular conditions, notices (delay, variation, suspension, termination), variation orders, EOT determinations, claim responses, subcontract packages, contract risk registers, dispute notices, settlement agreements.
- **Employers:** Contractors, developers, PSUs, PMCs, infrastructure concessionaires.
- **THE GATE:** The named contractual decision-maker. Under **FIDIC Red Book 1999** the **Engineer** — a named person or firm, not the Employer — issues the Interim Payment Certificate: **cl. 14.6**, within **28 days** of receiving the Statement and supporting documents, issued to the Employer; **cl. 14.7**, the Employer pays within **56 days** (the trigger point is drafted differently in commentaries and is routinely amended — one Indian project addendum replaces '56' with '30' in sub-cl. 14.7(b), [DFCCIL Addendum 6](https://dfccil.com/upload/Addendum_6_dated_17.11.2014__.pdf) — **read the Contract Data, do not assume**). Under **NEC** the equivalent named role is the **Project Manager**, who assesses the amount due — **I did not verify the NEC4 clause numbers in this session; NOT VERIFIED.** Under CPWD, the Engineer-in-Charge and the Divisional Officer are the named roles and the bill rests on MB entries (A3). The gate is that **only the named person's certificate creates the payment obligation** — a commercial manager's agreement does not.

### C2. Contracts Administrator
- **Indian titles:** Contracts Administrator; Contract Engineer; "Contracts Executive".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred 3339 / 4110 at the clerical end, 2149 at the engineering end.
- **Day to day:** The clock-keeper. Logs every notice in and out against its contractual time bar, maintains the correspondence and document registers, tracks the status of every variation, EOT and claim, issues the templated notices, and keeps the contract's registers audit-ready.
- **Deliverables:** Notice register with time-bar dates, variation register, claim register, correspondence log, document transmittal records, insurance and bond expiry tracker, contract compliance checklist.
- **Employers:** Contractors, PMCs, owners.
- **THE GATE:** None professional. The gate that matters is the **time bar itself**: a notice served late can extinguish an otherwise good entitlement (FIDIC's 28-day notice regime for claims being the usual example). That is a date arithmetic gate, and it is one of the places computation must refuse rather than estimate — see the final section.

### C3. Commercial Manager
- **Indian titles:** Commercial Manager; Head–Commercial; "Commercial & Contracts". In Indian contracting, "Commercial" often also owns purchase and stores, which is a different scope from the UK meaning.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred 1323 / 1219.
- **Day to day:** Owns margin. Reviews the bid's commercial assumptions, sets the subcontract and supply strategy, decides which claims to pursue and which to trade, runs the monthly commercial review (cost value reconciliation), manages cash collection and the final account, and carries the project's P&L to management.
- **Deliverables:** Cost-value reconciliation (CVR), margin forecast, claim strategy paper, subcontract procurement schedule, final account, risk and opportunity schedule, cash collection plan.
- **Employers:** Contractors above all; developers' commercial cells; PMCs.
- **THE GATE:** None statutory for the role. Where the employer is a company, the **CVR and the margin position feed revenue recognition**, so the figures become the auditor's and the board's gate: percentage-of-completion revenue depends on the cost-to-complete the commercial manager supplies. A deliberately optimistic cost-to-complete is a financial-reporting matter, not a commercial one.

### C4. Claims Consultant
- **Indian titles:** Claims Consultant; Claims Manager; "Contracts & Claims Specialist".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**.
- **Day to day:** Builds or defends the money claim. Establishes the contractual entitlement clause by clause, assembles the factual narrative from site records, quantifies prolongation cost, disruption, loss of productivity, idle plant and overhead, and writes the claim submission that an engineer, a DRB or a tribunal will read.
- **Deliverables:** Claim submissions with entitlement/causation/quantum sections, prolongation cost build-ups, disruption and loss-of-productivity analyses, global claim narratives, rebuttals, quantum expert reports, settlement positions.
- **Employers:** Contractors, owners, specialist claims firms (HKA, Secretariat, Accuracy, Exponent and local equivalents), law firms' construction practices.
- **THE GATE:** When the claim becomes an arbitration, the gate is who may decide it — see C6. For quantum presented as **expert evidence**, the gate is the tribunal's acceptance of the person as an expert, and the professional-body duty where the expert is RICS-regulated. For claims against government, there is also a pre-arbitral gate: CPWD practice routes disputes first to a **Dispute Redressal Committee** and then to arbitration under the contract's Clause 25 — a claim filed straight to arbitration can be thrown out for skipping it.

### C5. Delay Analyst / Forensic Planner
- **Indian titles:** Delay Analyst; Forensic Planner; "EOT Specialist"; "Planning & Claims".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**.
- **Day to day:** Proves or disproves that an event caused critical delay. Validates the baseline programme, reconstructs the as-built from site records, selects and runs a delay-analysis method, separates employer-risk from contractor-risk delay, addresses concurrency and float, and writes it up defensibly.
- **Deliverables:** Baseline validation report, as-built programme, delay analysis report, windows/time-slice analyses, impacted-as-planned or collapsed-as-built models, EOT entitlement schedule, concurrency and float opinion, critical path narratives.
- **Employers:** Claims consultancies, contractors, owners, law firms, arbitral appointments as programming expert.
- **THE GATE:** No licence. The governing methodology standard is the **SCL Delay and Disruption Protocol, 2nd edition (February 2017)**, which **removed** the 1st edition's preference for Time Impact Analysis where the assessment is made at a time distant from the event, added **as-planned vs as-built windows analysis** and **time slice windows analysis** as recognised methods, and instead sets out the factors for selecting the appropriate method ([Addleshaw Goddard summary of the 2nd ed.](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/globalassets/insights/real-estate/the-scl-delay-and-disruption-protocol-2nd-edition-february-2017-updated-and-improved.pdf); [Fenwick Elliott](https://www.fenwickelliott.com/research-insight/newsletters/international-quarterly/scl-delay-disruption-protocol-second-edition)). The 2nd edition also recognises that **true concurrent delay is rare**, and its **Core Principle 4** says the parties should deal with the time impact of Employer Risk Events as the work proceeds rather than "wait and see". **This matters for computation: the Protocol deliberately does not mandate one method, so "which method" is a judgement input, never a derivable output.**

### C6. Dispute / Arbitration Support
- **Indian titles:** Arbitration Support; Disputes Manager; "Litigation & Arbitration (Projects)"; expert and expert-support roles.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; legal practitioners inferred 2611.
- **Day to day:** Assembles the record: pleadings support, document discovery, chronologies, witness-statement drafting support, expert instruction, quantum and delay schedules, Scott Schedules, hearing bundles, and the party's own costs position.
- **Deliverables:** Statement of claim/defence annexures, Scott Schedule, agreed and disputed chronology, document bundles, expert reports (delay, quantum, technical), settlement/mediation position papers, award implementation schedules.
- **Employers:** Law firms, claims consultancies, contractors, PSUs, owners.
- **THE GATE — quote this one, it is the hardest in Indian construction disputes.** The **Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996, s.12(5)** read with the **Seventh Schedule** makes a person ineligible to be appointed arbitrator where his relationship with the parties, counsel or subject matter falls within the Schedule — including an **employee of a party** — and this operates **by operation of law**, waivable only by an **express agreement in writing after the dispute has arisen**. The Supreme Court in **Perkins Eastman Architects DPC v. HSCC (India) Ltd. (2020)** held that a person who is himself ineligible to act as arbitrator **cannot appoint** one either, extending *TRF Ltd. v. Energo Engineering (2017)* ([Maheshwari & Co.](https://www.maheshwariandco.com/blog/unilateral-appointment-of-arbitrator/); [JGU Mapping ADR](https://jgu.edu.in/mappingADR/can-a-person-who-is-ineligible-to-become-an-arbitrator-nominate-another-arbitrator)). This has demolished the standard PSU clause: the **Gauhati High Court** has held that appointing arbitrators from a panel of serving or retired railway officials contravenes s.12(5) and the Seventh Schedule ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/gauhati-high-court/gauhati-high-court-arbitral-tribunal-servingretired-railway-officers-violates-section-125-of-arbitration-act-261122)), and the **Delhi High Court** has terminated a court-appointed arbitrator's mandate under s.14 over past CPWD association ([K&K note](https://ksandk.com/litigation/delhi-hc-ends-arbitrators-mandate-over-past-cpwd-links/)). **Caveat and it is a live one:** sources record conflicting three-judge-bench positions on s.12(5)/Seventh Schedule and a referral on whether a Schedule-VII-ineligible person may appoint an arbitrator; **I did not find the final outcome — NOT VERIFIED, check the current Supreme Court position before relying on it.**

### C7. Insurance and Bonds Coordinator
- **Indian titles:** Insurance Coordinator; "Bank Guarantee / BG Cell"; Risk & Insurance Officer. In Indian contracting the BG desk is usually a named function because of the volume of guarantees in play.
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred 3321 (insurance representatives) or 4312.
- **Day to day:** Places and keeps alive every instrument the contract requires: CAR/EAR policy, third-party liability, workmen's compensation, marine/transit cover for imported plant, performance bank guarantee, advance-payment guarantee, retention-money BG, mobilisation BG. Tracks expiry and extension against the extended completion date, notifies claims within policy time limits, and gets guarantees released at the right milestone.
- **Deliverables:** Insurance schedule and policy register, certificates of insurance to the employer, BG register with expiry and extension status, claim notifications and loss files, bond release requests, premium allocation to projects.
- **Employers:** Contractors, developers, PSUs, insurance brokers.
- **THE GATE:** Insurance is written only by an **IRDAI-registered insurer** through a licensed intermediary, and a bank guarantee is issued only by a **bank**; neither can be substituted by an undertaking. ### F2. RERA Compliance / Escrow Coordinator — **the clearest statutory gate in this whole list**
- **Indian titles:** RERA Compliance Manager; RERA Coordinator; "Project Compliance (RERA)".
- **NCO-2015:** **NOT FOUND**; inferred 2411 / 2619.
- **Day to day:** Keeps the registered project lawful. Maintains the designated separate account, computes the withdrawal entitlement from the percentage of completion, collects the three professional certificates before each withdrawal, files the quarterly project updates on the authority's portal, gets the annual audit done, and keeps the architect/engineer/CA designations current with the authority.
- **Deliverables:** Separate-account reconciliation, withdrawal computation (70% rule and proportionate entitlement), the three certificates per withdrawal (commonly the state Forms 1/2/3), quarterly QPRs, annual audited statement and the CA's certificate on utilisation, allottee-wise collection and receipt records, project extension applications.

- **THE GATE — the provision and who certifies what.** **Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016, s.4(2)(l)(D)**: seventy per cent of the amounts realised for the real estate project from the allottees, from time to time, shall be deposited in a separate account to be maintained in a scheduled bank to cover the cost of construction and the land cost and shall be used only for that purpose. The **first proviso** requires that the promoter shall withdraw the amounts from the separate account, to cover the cost of the project, **in proportion to the percentage of completion of the project**. The **second proviso** permits withdrawal only after it is **certified by an engineer, an architect and a chartered accountant in practice** that the withdrawal is in proportion to the percentage of completion. The **third proviso** requires the promoter to get his accounts audited within six months after the end of every financial year by a **chartered accountant in practice**, and to produce a statement of accounts duly certified and signed by such CA, verifying that the amounts collected for a particular project have been utilised for that project and that the withdrawal has been in compliance with the proportion to the percentage of completion. In practice the division of labour is settled: the **architect certifies the percentage of physical completion** (Form 1), the **engineer certifies the actual cost incurred on the work done** (Form 2), and the **chartered accountant in practice issues the Form 3** certificate supporting the withdrawal (as described in [TaxGuru/ICAI-chapter practitioner material](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/rera-certificates-rerd-act-karnataka-rules.html) and [CA Vinay T, *Book on CA Certificates under RERD Act*, TaxGuru-hosted](https://cdn.taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Book-on-CA-Certificates-under-RERD-Act-_By-CA-Vinay-T.pdf)). **Sourcing caveat, stated plainly: indiacode.nic.in returns HTTP 403 to automated fetch, so the wording above is reconstructed from multiple consistent ICAI-chapter and practitioner sources that quote the provisos, not from the gazette. Verify against the Act before quoting it in a document that matters.** Three further points that are verified and operationally important: (i) commentators dispute whether **"in practice"** qualifies all three professionals or only the CA — this is genuinely unsettled; (ii) state rules add the requirement that the promoter **designate** the architect, engineer and CA and that they **cannot be changed without intimation to the Authority** (Kerala and Karnataka rules, per TaxGuru); (iii) **MahaRERA Circular 39/2021** provides that fresh certificates from the architect, engineer and CA are **not** required for each withdrawal unless the certified withdrawal limit is exhausted ([WIRC-ICAI material](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/law-updates/maharera-april-2022.pdf)). On penalty for excess withdrawal, sources conflict between **s.60** and **s.61** for the up-to-5%-of-estimated-project-cost penalty — **NOT RESOLVED; check the Act.**

# WHICH PARTS OF THIS WORK ARE PURE ARITHMETIC ON RULES

The question that decides what is computable. For each: the rule source, and whether the calculation is **fully determined** (same inputs, one answer, no judgement) or **underdetermined** (a judgement input must be supplied before arithmetic begins).

### 1. Measurement from drawings per IS 1200 — **PARTIALLY determined. The arithmetic is determined; the classification is not.**
- **Rule source:** IS 1200 Parts 1–28 (no Part 17), each part prescribing units, the order of measurement, and deduction and inclusion rules per trade ([BIS CED 44 index](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/manifest.ced.44.html)). Internationally **RICS NRM 2** (NRM 2021 suite; earlier editions withdrawn for new projects).
- **Determined:** once an item is correctly classified and its dimensions are read, the quantity is arithmetic — length × breadth × depth, deductions per the stated rule, rounded to the stated precision. Deduction rules are explicit and mechanical (openings above a stated area deducted, below it not).
- **Not determined:** (a) **which revision year of which part governs** — the contract must say, and parts have been revised (Part 5 and Part 1 show 2025 BIS entries, Part 6 a 2024 entry) so a wrong-year deduction rule gives a wrong-but-plausible quantity; (b) **classification** — whether a given element is measured under Part 2 Concrete, Part 5 Formwork, Part 8 Steelwork, or partly in each, is a reading of the item description against the standard; (c) **what the drawing actually shows** — a dimension inferred from a scaled drawing, an unresolved revision clash, or a detail "to be issued" is not an input, and arithmetic on it manufactures false precision. **Computation should refuse where the governing part/year is unstated, where the item description does not map to a single measurement rule, or where a dimension is scaled rather than figured.**

### 2. Rate analysis build-up (material + labour + plant + overhead + profit) — **DETERMINED given coefficients and basic rates; the coefficients and the OH&P percentage are inputs, not derivable.**
- **Rule source:** **CPWD Analysis of Rates for DSR (AOR Vol I, Vol II)** alongside DSR-2023 Vol I/II ([Railway Board adoption order](https://indianrailways.gov.in/railwayboard/uploads/directorate/civil_engg/2025/2025_07_03%20Adoption%20of%20CPWD%20Delhi%20Schedule%20of%20Rates-2023%20(2).pdf)); state SORs for state works.
- **Determined:** rate = Σ(material coefficient × basic rate) + Σ(labour coefficient × wage) + plant/T&P + (subtotal × OH&P%). Pure arithmetic once every term is given.
- **Not determined:** the coefficients and the OH&P percentage are published values that must be **looked up in the AOR for that item in that SOR edition**, not computed and not remembered. CPWD/DSR practice adds contractor's profit and overheads at **15%** (confirmed in Ladakh SOR-2024: "contractor's profit and overheads @ 15% has been adopted"), but a secondary worked example applies **22%** on a shuttering item, and private practice runs 18–25%. **So the OH&P percentage is item- and SOR-specific. A system that hardcodes 15% will be quietly wrong on some items and badly wrong on private work.** Basic rates are market inputs at a location and date. **Computation should refuse to produce a rate without a cited AOR item reference and edition, and must never infer a coefficient from a similar item.**

### 3. Abstract / BOQ pricing — **FULLY determined.**
- **Rule source:** the contract's own BOQ and schedule of rates.
- **Determined:** amount = quantity × rate per item; abstract = Σ amounts by section; totals roll up. Fully mechanical, and the one place arithmetic can be trusted end to end.
- **The only failure modes are structural, not arithmetic:** unit mismatch between quantity and rate (m² quantity against a per-m rate), a rate applied to the wrong item line, double-counting where an item appears in two sections, and rounding applied per line then re-applied at the total. These are checkable by rule — **a system should assert unit consistency and refuse on mismatch rather than multiply.**

### 4. Escalation / price-variation formulae — **DETERMINED in form, but the inputs are published indices with revision and base-year traps; and whether the clause applies at all is a contract question.**
- **Rule source:** **CPWD GCC Clause 10CC** for CPWD works; equivalent price-adjustment clauses in FIDIC (sub-clause 13.8 in the 1999 Red Book) and in NHAI/Railways/state forms.
- **Determined:** once the clause, the component weightages, the base indices and the current indices are fixed, the escalation amount is a formula. Courts describe 10CC as providing for escalation on account of increase or decrease in the prices of materials and wages.
- **Not determined:** (a) **whether the clause applies** — Delhi High Court records a party's argument that escalation under Clause 10CC applies only if the contract duration exceeds 18 months, with the court holding that applicability depends on the specific contract terms, and CAG has flagged 10CC being wrongly inserted into lump-sum contracts, one instance costing ₹6.41 crore of avoidable payment ([CAG Delhi civil report](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/old_reports/state/Delhi/2007/Civil/Civil_Delhi_2007/vol_1chap4.pdf)); (b) the **index series, its base year, and whether a figure is provisional or final** — indices get revised and rebased, and an escalation computed on a provisional index is a payment that will have to be reversed; (c) the **component weightages** are contract data. **Computation should refuse to escalate without the clause text, the named index series with base year, and the stated weightages — and should mark any amount computed on a provisional index as provisional.**

### 5. Earned value — **FULLY determined arithmetically; the single input it rests on is a judgement.**
- **Rule source:** standard EV formulae (CV = BCWP − ACWP; SV = BCWP − BCWS; CPI = BCWP/ACWP; SPI = BCWP/BCWS; EAC = BAC/CPI or BAC = ACWP + ETC depending on the chosen formula).
- **Determined:** every metric, once BCWS, BCWP and ACWP are given.
- **Not determined:** **BCWP — the physical percent complete — is an assessment, not a measurement**, except where the work is measured against a BOQ (in which case use the measured quantity and say so). The choice of EAC formula is also an assumption about whether past performance continues. **Computation should compute EV metrics freely but must never generate percent complete; and it should name which EAC formula it used, because the two give materially different answers.**

### 6. Delay analysis methods — **NOT determined. This is the least computable item on the list, and the Protocol says so.**
- **Rule source:** **SCL Delay and Disruption Protocol, 2nd edition (February 2017)**.
- **Why it is not determined:** the 2nd edition **deliberately removed** the 1st edition's preference for Time Impact Analysis where the assessment is distant from the event, added **as-planned vs as-built windows** and **time slice windows** as recognised methods, and in place of a preferred method **sets out the factors to be taken into account in selecting the most appropriate form of analysis** ([Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/globalassets/insights/real-estate/the-scl-delay-and-disruption-protocol-2nd-edition-february-2017-updated-and-improved.pdf); [Fenwick Elliott](https://www.fenwickelliott.com/research-insight/newsletters/international-quarterly/scl-delay-disruption-protocol-second-edition)). Different methods on the same facts give different EOT answers; that is a known property, not a defect.
- **What *is* determined:** once a method and a window structure are chosen, the mechanics are reproducible — in as-planned vs as-built windows, the critical path in each window is identified from contemporaneous data (baseline and updates) and critical delay is measured by comparing key dates along the contemporaneous or actual critical path against the corresponding planned dates in the baseline. Also mechanical: calendar arithmetic, non-working-day handling, and float computation from a given logic network.
- **Not determined:** method selection, window boundaries, baseline validity, which logic links are "as-built", float ownership, and **concurrency** — the 2nd edition recognises that **true concurrent delay is rare**, which means concurrency findings are contested conclusions, not arithmetic. **Computation must refuse to assert an EOT entitlement. It can run a named method on a stated network and report the result as method-dependent.**

### 7. Retention and release — **DETERMINED, and a trap because the rule is in the contract, not in a standard.**
- **Rule source:** the contract's payment clause; FIDIC uses a retention percentage and a limit of retention with release in halves at Taking-Over and at the Performance Certificate.
- **Determined:** retention deducted = certified amount × retention % until cumulative retention reaches the stated limit, then nil; release per the stated milestones.
- **Not determined and routinely got wrong:** the **limit of retention** (retention stops accruing once the cap is hit — missing this over-deducts for the rest of the job), whether retention is computed on gross certified value or net of materials-on-site and advances, whether a retention BG has been substituted, and **what event triggers each half-release** (practical completion vs defects-liability expiry vs certificate issue). **Computation should refuse without the retention %, the cap, the base, and the named release events.**

### 8. Liquidated damages — **arithmetically determined, legally NOT determined. The most dangerous "computable" item here.**
- **Rule source:** the contract's LD/compensation-for-delay clause (CPWD GCC Clause 2 is the Indian public-works archetype — **I was unable to verify its text in this session; NOT FOUND**), governed by **s.74 of the Indian Contract Act 1872**.
- **Determined:** LD = rate per period × periods of culpable delay, capped at the stated maximum.
- **Not determined, and this is the whole point:** (a) **periods of culpable delay** depend on the EOT determination, which per item 6 is not computable; (b) **enforceability** is contested law. **ONGC v. Saw Pipes (2003)** held the tribunal wrong to refuse LD for want of proof of actual loss, treating a stipulated sum as enforceable where not unreasonable; **Kailash Nath Associates v. DDA (2015)** held that compensation under s.74 is payable **only where damage or loss is caused by the breach**. Commentators read the two as turning on whether loss is genuinely incapable of proof (pre-estimate carries weight) or readily provable and simply not proved (court may limit the award), and note that the proof requirement is fact-specific and varies with the nature of the contract ([SCC Online analysis](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/07/27/section-74-proof-paradox-liquidated-damages-indian-contracts/); [Mondaq, *Curious case of Section 74*](https://www.mondaq.com/india/contracts-and-commercial-law/950450/curious-case-of-section-74-of-the-indian-contract-act)). A Bombay High Court ruling reportedly reduced an award for failure to prove actual loss. **So: a computed LD figure is a contractual claim, never an established liability. Computation must label it as such and must not net it against a payment due without the determination that authorises it.**

### 9. GST on a works contract — **DETERMINED once classification and rate entry are fixed; both are the hard part.**
- **Rule source:** CGST Act **s.2(119)** (definition of works contract); **Notification 11/2017-CTR** entry **3(ii)** at **18%**; the concessional entries introduced by **20/2017, 24/2017, 31/2017-CTR**; **Notification 03/2022-CTR dated 13.07.2022** modifying the concessional entry such that 18% applies to works executed and billed thereafter.
- **Determined:** tax = taxable value × rate, split CGST/SGST or IGST by place of supply; the computation itself is trivial.
- **Not determined:** (a) **classification** — works contract vs composite supply of goods vs pure services, with a long line of advance rulings on exactly this; (b) **which entry and which rate as at the date of supply** — the 2022 change means the *same work* carries 12% or 18% depending on when it was executed and billed, so **the date is part of the input, not a detail**; (c) **time of supply** against RA bill certification; (d) **ITC eligibility**, including the blocked credits for immovable property. **Computation should refuse to state a rate without the entry number and the effective date, and should never carry a rate forward across a notification date.**

### 10. BOCW cess — percentage **DETERMINED (1%)**; **base NOT determined without Rule 3.**
- **Rule source:** **Cess Act 1996 s.3(1)** — not exceeding 2%, not less than 1% of the cost of construction incurred by an employer, at the notified rate; **notified at 1%** (CAG: GoI notified September 1996 at one per cent); **"cost of construction" defined in Rule 3 of the Cess Rules 1998**.
- **Determined:** cess = cost of construction × 1%.
- **Not determined:** **what goes into "cost of construction."** That is Rule 3's job, and **I did not retrieve Rule 3's text — it is NOT VERIFIED here.** Whether land cost, client-supplied material, plant, consultancy, and GST are in or out of the base changes the number materially, and it is the single most litigated element of the levy. **Computation must refuse to compute cess from a project cost figure of unknown composition. The percentage is the easy half and the easy half is not the risk.**

# WHERE A NUMBER CAN BE WRONG AND NOBODY NOTICES

The places where a mistake is both easy to make and expensive, and where the error produces a *plausible* number rather than a failure. These are where computation must refuse, not guess.

**1. The IS 1200 part/revision in force.** A deduction rule from the wrong revision year gives a quantity that is wrong by a few per cent across every item of a trade. Nothing flags it; the BOQ adds up perfectly. Compounded by the fact that several parts have been revised recently and some date from the 1970s.

**2. A unit mismatch between quantity and rate.** m² against a per-m rate, MT against per-kg, cum against per-sqm. The abstract totals, the total looks like money, and the error is one or three orders of magnitude. This is the one thing a machine should check unconditionally and refuse on.

**3. Retention past the cap.** Retention keeps being deducted after the limit of retention is reached. Each bill looks normal, the contractor's cash position quietly erodes, and it surfaces only at the final account — by which time it is an argument about interest as well as principal.

**4. The OH&P percentage treated as a constant.** 15% is the CPWD/DSR convention, but it is not universal — item-level exceptions exist in the same SOR, and private work runs 18–25%. A hardcoded markup is wrong silently on every item it touches.

**5. Theoretical consumption in a material reconciliation.** The coefficient comes from the SOR/AOR item. Use a nearby item's coefficient and the reconciliation shows a shortage or excess that does not exist — and in most Indian contracts an unreconciled shortage is **recovered from the RA bill at a punitive rate**. The recovery looks correctly computed. The coefficient was wrong.

**6. Escalation on a provisional or rebased index.** Indices get revised and rebased. An escalation paid on a provisional figure, or on a series whose base year differs from the contract's base, is a payment that must later be reversed from a contractor who has already spent it. And one layer above the arithmetic: CAG has recorded **₹6.41 crore of avoidable payment** from Clause 10CC being applied where it should not have been — the formula was right and the clause did not apply.

**7. Percent complete feeding three different consequences.** The same assessed percentage drives EV reporting, revenue recognition under percentage-of-completion, and — for a registered real-estate project — the **lawful withdrawal entitlement from the RERA separate account**. An optimistic percentage is a management opinion in the first case, a financial-reporting matter in the second, and a **statutory contravention attracting a penalty of up to 5% of estimated project cost** in the third. The number looks identical in all three places.

**8. The BOCW cess base.** 1% is certain. What the 1% applies to is defined by Rule 3 of the Cess Rules 1998 and is the most contested element of the levy. Compute 1% of an undefined "project cost" and you get a confident number that an assessment will not accept.

**9. The GST rate across a notification date.** The same scope of work carries 12% or 18% depending on when it was executed and billed, because Notification 03/2022-CTR (13.07.2022) moved the concessional entry. Carrying one rate across the whole contract produces a consistent, wrong tax position — recoverable from the contractor with interest, usually after the margin is gone.

**10. A notice served one day late.** Not an arithmetic error at all — a calendar error. Contractual time bars extinguish entitlement regardless of merit. The claim is worth its full value right up to the deadline and nothing the day after, and nothing in the file changes to show it.

**11. An award from a tribunal that could not lawfully be constituted.** Years of claim work can be set aside because the arbitrator fell within the **Seventh Schedule** (s.12(5)) or was appointed by someone himself ineligible (*Perkins Eastman*). This has already voided panels of serving and retired railway officers and ended a mandate over past CPWD association. The merits were never the problem.

**12. A measurement recorded in the Measurement Book.** The deepest one. The MB is a legal record of fact, test-checked and signed by named officers; once it is signed, it is the basis of payment and the basis of audit. A wrong entry is not a draft — correcting it later requires explaining why the original was signed. This is the strongest argument in the whole list for refusing to generate a measurement that no one has verified against the work.

**13. An LD figure netted against a payment.** LD is arithmetically trivial and legally contested (*Saw Pipes* vs *Kailash Nath*), and its input — culpable delay — is not computable. A system that computes LD and deducts it has converted a contested claim into an executed recovery.

**14. The thing I kept hitting while researching this, and it generalises.** Four of the governing documents here — NCO-2015 Vol II-B, the CPWD Works Manual 2022 body text, the RERA gazette text, Rule 3 of the Cess Rules — were not retrievable from the open web in this session, while *commentary about them* was abundant and consistent. Consistent secondary commentary is how a wrong number becomes unanimous. For anything that gates a payment or a statutory filing, the primary document has to be read.
