## The findings that shape every film desk
### 1. The creative and legal signatures are human; software prepares, tests and tracks
Ladder as before: **A** admin/prepare, **B** assist (draft / check / estimate for a named person), **C** reserved.
Reserved here: creative decisions (script acceptance, casting, edit, final mix, grade), the director's cut, signing talent and
crew contracts, chain-of-title sign-off, rating/censor submission as the producer, music licence grants, payment of crew/talent,
insurance and completion-bond decisions, and **any use of a real person's face or voice** without that person's recorded consent
(guild and law now require it). The AI proposes, schedules, budgets, drafts, finds problems and produces assets; people decide.

### 2. Film is project-based: the money is in budgeting, scheduling and rights
A film is a one-off company with a budget, a shooting schedule, hundreds of daily decisions, call sheets, permits, location
agreements, music clearances, deliverables to dozens of buyers, and a rights chain that decides whether anybody can release it.
Scheduling (day-out-of-days, shoot order, weather cover), budgeting (above/below the line, contingency), cost reports and
rights/clearance tracking are exactly what a logged, rule-based AI Employee does best.

### 3. Generative media is the new edge and the new risk
Hoola can already make pictures, clips, music, voices, dubbed audio and talking avatars. In film this lets small makers
do previs, animatics, storyboards, pitch trailers, VFX plates, ADR, dubbing, subtitles and posters at low cost. Rules from the guilds
and the law: consent and compensation for digital replicas, no deceptive deepfakes, label synthetic media, license-clean
training and source material, human authorship for copyright claims in many jurisdictions. Every generative employee below carries
the Hoola approval card and a consent register.

### 4. Distribution and marketing are 40-60% of the lifecycle
Festivals, sales agents, platform deliverables (QC specs, captions, audio stems), release calendars, trailer and poster tests, social
campaigns and audience data. Much of it is checklist-and-spec work: tier A.

## NEVER an AI Employee (tier C)
Sign a contract or release as a person; decide a casting, final cut, grade or mix; use a real person's face/voice/body
without a recorded consent and scope; present synthetic footage as real or a fake endorsement; fabricate a crew credit or
statement; accept a rating/censor certificate or submit as the producer without them; license music or grant rights;
pay crew/talent/vendors on its own; file legal takedowns or lawsuits in a rights holder's name; scrape or republish
paywalled/pirated material; train on or reproduce a copyrighted work beyond fair use; leak or share unreleased footage;
make creative decisions that take credit from writers, directors or performers.
