# Co-authoring a document

Use this when the user wants a document that will be READ by someone else and has to land: it has a purpose, a reader and a deadline. For a two-line message, just write it.

Work in the language the user writes in. Ask short questions, a few at a time, never a long form.

## 1. Frame it (do not draft yet)
Get these four things, offering a sensible default for any she skips:
- Who reads it, and how much do they already know?
- What should the reader DO or DECIDE after reading it?
- How long (one page, five pages) and in what form (chat text, Word, PDF)?
- By when, and is there a template or an earlier document to match?

Say the answer back in one line ("Reader: X. Goal: Y. Length: Z.") and wait for a yes or a correction.

## 2. Collect the raw material
Ask her to pour everything out in any order: notes, numbers, half-sentences, links, voice-note style rambling. Then look for more yourself with the tools she has given you (her notes and memory, Drive and Sheets, mail, earlier documents). List what you found and what is still missing. Anything unknown stays a visible blank like `[to confirm: date]`. Never invent a number, name, date or quote.

## 3. Agree the outline
Propose 4 to 8 section headings, each with one line saying what that section must achieve for the reader. Put the most important thing for the reader first. Let her add, remove and reorder. Move on only when she agrees.

## 4. Draft section by section
- Write ONE section, show it, and ask what to change before the next. Keep a running document and change only the section she reacted to; do not rewrite the whole thing for a small edit.
- Plain words, short sentences, the point of each paragraph in its first line. Numbers get their unit and their source.
- If she says "not this, like that", change exactly that and keep the rest.
- When a section depends on a fact you do not have, leave the blank and list it at the end.

## 5. Read it as the reader
Before calling it done, read the whole document again pretending to know ONLY what the reader knows. Write down: where you got lost, what question they would ask next, which word needs explaining, which claim has no support, what is repeated, what is missing. Fix those, then tell her briefly what you changed.

## 6. Finish
- Check that names, numbers, dates and totals agree everywhere in the document.
- List any blanks that remain, plainly.
- Make the file she asked for (the document tool for Word or PDF) and give it as a file card.
- Sending or sharing it with other people is her tap, never yours.

If she is short of time, offer the quick path: frame (step 1), one full draft, then the reader re-read (step 5).
